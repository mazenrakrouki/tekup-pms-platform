\chapter{Release 3 --- Financial Management and Governance}

\section*{Introduction}
This release delivers the value for which the platform was commissioned: replacing the financial
logic of the Excel workbook. Sprint~6 records the revenue side (billing milestones, payments,
amendments) and the mission expenses that weigh on the margin. Sprint~7 adds the governance
registers that document the life of a project. Sprint~8 closes the loop with the internal quote and
the indicator engine, which consumes everything built since Release~1.

% =====================================================================
\section{Sprint 6 --- Billing and Missions}

\subsection{Sprint Objective}
Record what the project earns and what it spends outside labour: billing milestones with their
payments, contractual amendments, and business-trip costs.

\subsection{Functional Scope}
This sprint covers user stories US-15 to US-18: defining billing milestones, recording payments,
recording amendments, and recording missions with their expenses.

\subsection{Features Implemented}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Billing milestones expressed as a percentage of the contract, with a lifecycle.
  \item Payments recorded against a milestone.
  \item Contractual amendments adjusting the revised budget.
  \item Missions with multi-currency cost components and automatic total.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Technical Realisation}

\subsubsection{Billing milestones}
A milestone belongs to one project and carries a label, a percentage of the contract, an amount, a
due date and a status. The amount is derived from the effective budget and the percentage, so that
an amendment automatically propagates to the milestones still to be invoiced.

The structural rule inherited from the workbook is enforced in the service layer: the \textbf{sum of
milestone percentages may not exceed 100~\%} of the contract. Attempting to exceed it is rejected
rather than silently accepted, which the spreadsheet allowed.

\subsubsection{Milestone lifecycle}
A milestone moves through three states: \texttt{PREVU} (planned), \texttt{FACTURE} (invoiced) and
\texttt{PAYE} (paid). Invoicing records the invoice date; a payment recorded against an invoiced
milestone moves it to paid. Payments are kept as individual rows rather than as a single settled
amount, so that partial settlements remain visible.

\subsubsection{Amendments}
An amendment carries a reference, an amount and an impact on the sold workload. It is the
\textbf{single writer} of the revised budget introduced in Sprint~3: no other operation may modify
it. This constraint is what keeps the contractual and financial history coherent.

\subsubsection{Missions and multi-currency}
A mission belongs to a project and an employee, and its total cost is the sum of its components ---
per diem, ticket, accommodation, transport, stamp. Because a mission may be incurred in a currency
different from the reporting currency, each component is converted \textbf{before} summation using
the rate at the mission date. Summing first and converting afterwards would mix currencies and
distort the total.

\section{Sprint 7 --- Project Governance}

\subsection{Sprint Objective}
Deliver the governance registers of the reference workbook: risks, deliverables, stakeholders and
change requests, so that the qualitative life of a project is documented alongside its financial
figures.

\subsection{Functional Scope}
This sprint covers user stories US-19 to US-21.

\subsection{Features Implemented}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Risk register with probability, impact, status and mitigation plan.
  \item Deliverable register with due date and delivery status.
  \item Stakeholder register with role and influence.
  \item Change request register with priority and decision status.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Technical Realisation}
The four registers share the same shape: each is composed by the project, is governed by a small
business enumeration, and is edited by the project manager. Their value lies less in their
individual complexity than in being attached to the project rather than kept in a separate
document, which is what the spreadsheet process could not guarantee.

The \textbf{deliverable register} carries a functional responsibility beyond documentation: the
delivery rate used by the indicator engine of Sprint~8 is derived from it, as the ratio of
delivered and validated deliverables to planned deliverables. This is why deliverables carry a
controlled status rather than a free-text field.

\section{Sprint 8 --- Internal Quote and Indicator Engine}

\subsection{Sprint Objective}
Deliver the economic baseline of a project, the internal quote, and the engine that computes
the earned-value indicators, together with the dashboards that expose them.

\subsection{Functional Scope}
This sprint covers user stories US-22 to US-25: defining the internal quote, computing the
indicators automatically, freezing a monthly review, and providing the portfolio dashboard.

\subsection{Features Implemented}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Internal quote structured by section, with restricted access.
  \item Automatic computation of the earned-value indicators.
  \item Frozen monthly review preserving the official state of the project.
  \item Per-project and portfolio dashboards.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Technical Realisation}

\subsubsection{The internal quote}
The internal quote is the economic baseline of the project: line items grouped by section (fees,
expenses, other expenses), each carrying a sold quantity and unit price on the revenue side, and
an internal quantity and unit cost on the cost side.

Two design decisions govern this module. First, access is restricted by a dedicated permission,
because unit costs and margins are commercially sensitive. Second, \textbf{amounts and margins are
never stored}: they are recomputed at read time from quantities, unit values and the exchange rate.
Persisting them would duplicate sensitive figures and risk divergence between the stored value and
its inputs.

\subsubsection{The hybrid indicator engine}
The engine implements the architecture decided in Chapter~3. On each read it recomputes, from live
data, the indicators of the reference workbook:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Actual cost} --- consumed man-days valued at the cost rate of their own year;
  \item \textbf{ETC} --- remaining man-days valued the same way;
  \item \textbf{EAC} --- actual cost plus ETC;
  \item \textbf{Production revenue} --- contract total weighted by the earned value percentage;
  \item \textbf{Accrued income} --- production revenue minus the invoiced total;
  \item \textbf{Margin} --- effective budget minus EAC, in value and in percentage;
  \item \textbf{Drift} --- sold workload minus consumed minus remaining.
\end{itemize}

Nothing is written during this computation: a dashboard read has no side effect.

\subsubsection{The frozen monthly review}
The earned value percentage is a judgement, not a measurement: it is entered by the project manager
during the monthly review. Submitting it creates a \textbf{snapshot} that freezes the indicators of
that month, together with the highlights recorded by the manager.

This is the digital equivalent of the monthly column of the statistics sheet: live figures for
steering, frozen figures for history. A snapshot is immutable, so the history of past reviews
cannot be rewritten by a later correction.

\subsection{Sprint Outcome}
The platform now reproduces the financial logic of the Excel workbook, automatically and under
access control. The functional objective of the project is met: a project manager obtains their
indicators without opening a spreadsheet, and a director sees the whole portfolio.

\subsection{Transition to the Next Sprint}
The functional scope is complete. What remains is to make the platform pleasant, verified and
deployable, the object of Release~4.

\section*{Conclusion}
Release~3 delivered the core value of PMS: billing and expenses, the governance registers, the
internal quote and above all the indicator engine that replaces the two thousand formulas of the
reference workbook. The hybrid design, live computation for steering and frozen snapshots for
history, reproduces the strengths of the spreadsheet while removing its fragility.
